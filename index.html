GPS Directorate01
2016–2017Space & Missile Systems CenterDefense acquisition

Modernizing the system the world uses to know where—and when—it is.

Mission context

GPS is a global positioning, navigation, and timing utility. Its timing signals synchronize communications, financial networks, transportation, and military operations. The organization then known as the Space and Missile Systems Center—reorganized as Space Systems Command in 2021—was the DoD acquisition center responsible for fielding the satellites, control segment, and user equipment.

My role

On the GPS Program Executive Officer staff, I helped govern a $32B portfolio across acquisition categories, supported $1.9B in GPS III funding and the $5.6B OCX next-generation control segment, and shaped a $100M gap-filler strategy. The job was translating technical risk into decisions senior leaders, Congress, and delivery teams could act on.

Nuclear detection02
2014–2016USNDS ground systemSandia · Los Alamos

Turning a distant physical event into trusted, near-real-time national warning.

Why the system matters

The United States Nuclear Detonation Detection System combines sensors carried in space with ground processing to detect, locate, and characterize nuclear detonations. Its lineage reaches to Vela and test-ban monitoring. Sandia and Los Alamos brought national-laboratory science to sensor phenomenology and detection; operational ground systems correlate the raw observations so analysts and national leaders receive actionable information.

My role

I led the $20M-a-year ground-system program spanning nine global sites and multiple security organizations. We implemented the Risk Management Framework, forecast 20% lifecycle savings, and delivered a $3M modernization 30 days early. The real product was not hardware alone—it was confidence that an extraordinary event would become a reliable decision signal.

Overhead intelligence03
2017–2021NRO · Air Force · SAICOPIR & GEOINT

From sensing overhead to intelligence a warfighter can actually use.

The joint enterprise

The NRO builds and operates reconnaissance spacecraft; NGA and other mission partners interpret and distribute the resulting collections; the military services turn those products into operational advantage. Today, NGA and the U.S. Space Force continue to formalize joint collection and tactical sensing. That chain is only as useful as its ability to move data across organizational and security boundaries.

My role

At the NRO, I led a 55-person, $50M+ portfolio that integrated OPIR data, including 15 nontraditional feeds, raising available mission data by 38%. We took a cross-domain platform from zero to a $10M deployment, then secured a $26M follow-on. At SAIC, I carried that perspective into architecture studies across USSF, MDA, NGA, and the Intelligence Community.
